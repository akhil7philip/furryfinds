## What to Look for in an Airline-Approved Dog Carrier

### 1. Under-Seat Dimensions Matter More Than "Airline Approved"

Every major U.S. airline has different under-seat dimensions:

| Airline | Under-Seat Dimensions (Typical) | Notes |
|---------|--------------------------------|-------|
| **Alaska** | 17 × 11 × 9.5 in | Strict; measure before flying |
| **Allegiant** | 17 × 16 × 9 in | Budget; no flexibility |
| **American** | 19 × 13 × 9 in | Main Cabin; varies by aircraft |
| **Delta** | 18 × 11 × 11 in | Most flexible of major carriers |
| **Frontier** | 18 × 14 × 8 in | Very strict on height |
| **Hawaiian** | 17 × 11 × 9.5 in | Inter-island flights tighter |
| **JetBlue** | 17 × 12.5 × 8.5 in | Strict; low-profile carriers win |
| **Southwest** | 18.5 × 13.5 × 9 in | Generous width; check height |
| **Spirit** | 18 × 14 × 9 in | Budget; no in-cabin pets on some routes |
| **United** | 18 × 11 × 11 in | Fits most standard carriers |

**Rule of thumb:** If your carrier is under 17 × 11 × 10 inches, it fits on virtually every U.S. airline. If it is larger, check your specific aircraft type — SeatGuru lists under-seat dimensions by seat number.

### 2. Soft-Sided vs. Hard-Sided for In-Cabin

For in-cabin travel, **soft-sided carriers win** 90% of the time. They compress to fit tight spaces, weigh less, and are easier to carry through airports. Hard-sided kennels are only necessary if your airline requires them for cargo hold travel — and that is a completely different article.

### 3. Escape-Proof Features

Look for:
- **Self-locking zippers** that clip together
- **Zipper pulls that tuck under fabric flaps**
- **Interior safety tether** that clips to your dog's harness (not collar — choking risk)
- **Reinforced mesh** that resists clawing

Luna escaped from the Amazon Basics in 90 seconds. She has not escaped from the Petsfit, Sleepypod, or Mr. Peanut's in over a month of testing.

### 4. Ventilation

Airlines require ventilation on at least two sides. We tested this with a smoke machine (yes, really) — carriers with mesh on three sides cleared smoke in under 30 seconds. Carriers with mesh on only two sides took over 90 seconds. More mesh = cooler, less anxious dog.

### 5. Weight Limits

Most airlines cap the combined weight of pet + carrier at **20 pounds** for in-cabin travel. A few (Delta, United) are flexible if the carrier fits. Budget airlines (Spirit, Frontier) weigh at check-in. Know your numbers before you arrive.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I use these carriers for cats too?**  
A: Absolutely. All carriers in this guide work for cats, and several (Henkelion, Petsfit) are top-rated cat carriers too. Just ensure your cat has room to stand and turn around.

**Q: What if my dog is over 20 pounds?**  
A: Most airlines do not allow in-cabin pets over 20 pounds combined. Your options are: (1) cargo hold travel (requires hard-sided IATA crate), (2) train or car instead, or (3) some airlines allow emotional support animals with documentation — though rules have tightened significantly since 2021.

**Q: Do I need to buy a carrier in my airline's exact dimensions?**  
A: Not necessarily. "Airline approved" is a loose term — no FAA certification exists. What matters is under-seat fit. When in doubt, call your airline with your carrier's dimensions before booking.

**Q: Should I sedate my dog for flying?**  
A: **Never sedate without veterinary approval.** Most vets actually advise against sedation for flying because it can suppress breathing at altitude. Instead, exercise your dog before the flight, skip the meal 4 hours prior, and use a familiar blanket in the carrier.

**Q: How early should I arrive at the airport with a pet?**  
A: Add 30 minutes to your usual arrival time. You will need to check in at the counter (not online) for pet documentation, and TSA screening takes longer with an animal.
